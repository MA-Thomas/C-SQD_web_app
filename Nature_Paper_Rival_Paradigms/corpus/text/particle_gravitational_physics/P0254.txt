Heavy quark potential in STU and AdS 5–Reissner–Nordstrom
backgrounds
Wenxing Chenga, Zi-qiang Zhangb

Abstract In this paper, we study the heavy quark poten-
tial in STU and AdS5-Reissner–Nordstrom backgrounds. We
perform the analysis by extremizing the worldsheet of the
open string attached to the heavy quark and its antiquark
located at the boundary of these geometries. We ﬁnd that
the heavy quark potential is strongly dependent on the black
hole charges. In particular, for both backgrounds, increasing
the charge leads to increased potential, thus weakening the
interaction between heavy quarks. Taking it one step further,
we can infer that the inclusion of chemical potential weakens
the interaction between heavy quarks in quark gluon plasma.
1 Introduction
Heavy quark potential, the potential energy function that
describes the interaction between two heavy quarks, is an
important quantity in gauge theories. It also has great phe-
nomenological relevance in connection with the heavy ion
collision experiments at the Relativistic Heavy Ion Collider
(RHIC) and Large Hadron Collider (LHC), since the melting
of heavy quarkonium, e.g, (c¯c), is commonly considered one
of the main experimental signatures for quark gluon plasma
(QGP) formation in these experiments [1]. However, numer-
ous studies have shown that QGP is strongly coupled and
behaves like an ultralow-viscosity liquid [ 2,3]. Therefore,
perturbative quantum chromodynamics (QCD) is not very
well suited for studying the heavy quark potential of QGP.
In other words, one needs non-perturbative techniques, such
as the anti-de Sitter/conformal ﬁeld theory (AdS/CFT) cor-
respondence.
The AdS/CFT correspondence, sometimes called gauge/
gravity duality, is a conjectured relationship between the

last two decades, the AdS/CFT has yielded many important
insights for studying different aspects of QGP (see [ 7–9]

temperature (vacuum potential). Therein, the vacuum poten-

temporal Wilson loop [11–13], and the results indicate that
it is of Coulomb type. Subsequently, the ﬁnite-temperature
heavy quark potential is obtained [14,15] from the correlator
of two Polyakov loops, and the results show that it starts out
at small L (the separation distance between the quark and
antiquark) close to the vacuum potential but becomes zero
above a certain critical L, at which the potential has a kink.
After [10,14,15], there have been many attempts at address-
ing heavy quark potential from the AdS/CFT. For example,
the sub-leading-order corrections to the potential were stud-

found in [25–40].

would like to consider another important quantity of QGP,
i.e., the heavy quark potential. We want to know whether the
results of the heavy quark potential calculated in the STU and
AdS-RN backgrounds are consistent. In addition, we want
to understand how the chemical potential affects the heavy
quark potential. This is the aim of the paper.

4 Summary and discussion
The heavy quark potential is an important quantity in the
study of QGP. In this paper, we have studied the heavy quark
potential in STU and AdS-RN backgrounds by calculating
the NG action of a string whose worldsheet is attached to
the contour related to the rectangular Wilson loop. We found
that the potential is strongly dependent on the black hole
charges. In particular, for both backgrounds, increasing the
charge leads to an increase in the potential.
Let us explain the physical meanings of the results. Since
the black hole charge increases the heavy quark potential, one
can say that the presence of a black hole charge weakens the
attraction between the heavy quarks. Taking it one step fur-
ther, the inclusion of chemical potential weakens the interac-
tion between heavy quarks in QGP. Interestingly, it has been
argued that the magnetic ﬁeld [18], angular momentum [19],
and anisotropy [20] also increase the heavy quark potential.
However, it should be noted that for the STU background,
when the charge is large, the potential is positive for the
three-charge black hole, implying that these results may not
make sense. An analogous situation was observed in the cal-
culation of the shear viscosity and the jet quenching param-
eter for the three-charge black hole. In was shown [ 44] that
the shear viscosity quickly decreases (close to zero) as the
charge increases. Also, the jet quenching parameter increases
to inﬁnity as the charge increases [48,49]. Therefore, one can
infer that, in comparison with the one- and two-charge black
holes, the three-charge black hole is perhaps a special case,
and caution is required. Further, we can predict that the three-
charge black hole may not be used for studying QGP.
Finally, combining our results with those of others [ 45–
49], we would like to provide a brief conclusion about the
shear viscosity, jet quenching parameter, and heavy quark
potential under the inﬂuence of chemical potential. The
analysis of these quantities from the STU and AdS-RN
backgrounds shows that the inclusion of chemical poten-
tial reduces shear viscosity, enhances quark energy loss, and
weakens the interaction between heavy quarks.
