Abstract We propose to study thea0(980) and the/Lambda1(1670)
resonances in the /Lambda1+
c → π+η/Lambda1decay via the ﬁnal-state
interactions of the π+η and η/Lambda1pairs. The weak interaction
part proceeds through the c quark decay process: c(ud) →
(s + u + ¯d)(ud), while the hadronization part takes place in
two mechanisms, differing in how the quarks from the weak
decay combines into π+η/Lambda1with a q ¯q with the quantum
numbers of the vacuum. Because the ﬁnalπ+ηand η/Lambda1states
are in pure isospin I = 1 and I = 0 combinations, the
/Lambda1+
c → π+η/Lambda1decay can be an ideal process to study the
a0(980) and /Lambda1(1670) resonances. Describing the ﬁnal-state
interaction in the chiral unitary approach, we ﬁnd that the
π+η and η/Lambda1invariant mass distributions, up to an arbitrary
normalization, show clear cusp and peak structures, which
can be associated with thea0(980) and /Lambda1(1670) resonances,
respectively. The proposed mechanism can provide valuable
information on the nature of these resonances and can in
principle be tested by facilities such as BEPCII.
1 Introduction
Understanding the nature of mesons and baryons has always
been one of the most challenging topics in hadron physics.
The new observations [1–7] have challenged the conventional
wisdom that mesons are made of quark–antiquark pairs and
baryons are composed of three quarks at low energies. In
this respect, it is not surprising that both the a0(980) state
and the /Lambda1(1670) have generated a lot of interests in their
true nature. The a0(980) has been suggested of being either
a q ¯q, a tetraquark state, a meson–meson molecule, a glueball,
or a dynamically generated state [8]. Similarly, the/Lambda1(1670)
has been found to be consistent with both a naive three-quark
picture and a molecular picture dynamically generated from
η/Lambda1and ¯KN interactions.
a e-mail: lisheng.geng@buaa.edu.cn
In the chiral unitary approach, thea0(980) state was shown
to be dynamically generated from the interaction of ¯KK and
πη treated as coupled channels in isospin I = 1[ 9,10]. It
decays intoπηin s-wave with a total decay width around 170
MeV . The pole of thea0(980) is strongly tied to the coupled
channels and it disappears if either theπηor the K ¯K channel
is discarded. The closeness of its pole to the K ¯K threshold
and its strong coupling toK ¯K have led to the suggestion that
the a0(980) state might be a cusp effect [ 9,10]. As a result,
the a0(980) total decay width increases very fast as its mass
increases. For instance, Ref. [11] has claimed a large width
for the a0(980) around 200 MeV , where the a0(980) state
was studied within a realistic meson-exchange model for the
ππ and πη interactions.
On the other hand, in Ref. [ 12], it was claimed that both
the a0(980) and thea0(1450) resonance emerge from a single
q ¯q seed state. This state interacts with other mesons, giving
rise to meson–meson (MM) loop contributions to the corre-
sponding mass. These contributions shift the pole of the seed
state to higher energies, turning into the a0(1450).F o rt h e
a0(980), the meson cloud eats up the original seed, becoming
the largest component [12] (see also Refs. [13–16]). By now
it is commonly accepted that the a0(980) is not a standard
q ¯q state but an extraordinary state [17].
For the/Lambda1(1670) hyperon resonance, based on the analysis
of the available high precision data onK− p → η/Lambda1reaction,
it was argued to be a three-quark state [ 18]. Such a conclu-
sion is also supported in the study of the K− p → π0/Sigma10
reaction at low energies within the chiral quark model [ 19].
However, in the chiral unitary approach, the /Lambda1(1670) reso-
nance can be dynamically generated from thes-wave meson
baryon interaction in the strangenessS =− 1 sector [20,21].
Experimentally, the /Lambda1(1670) resonance is known to have a
strong coupling to theη/Lambda1channel [22]. Hence, it is expected
that the η/Lambda1production is dominated by formation of the
intermediate /Lambda1(1670) resonance.

hadronic resonances, some of which are subjects of intense
debate about their nature [8,24,25]. In addition, those weak

4 Conclusions
In the present work we have studied theπ
+ηand η/Lambda1invari-
ant mass distributions in the /Lambda1+
c → π+η/Lambda1decay to under-
stand better the a0(980) and /Lambda1(1670) resonances. The weak
interaction part is dominated by the c quark decay pro-
cess: c(ud) → (s + u + ¯d)(ud), while the hadronization
part can take place in two different mechanisms. In the ﬁrst
one, the sud cluster picks up a q ¯q pair from the vacuum
123


--- Page 6 ---
496 Page 6 of 7 Eur. Phys. J. C (2016) 76 :496
and hadronizes into a meson–baryon pair, while the u ¯d pair
from the weak decay turns into a π+. In the second mech-
anism, the sud cluster hadronizes into a /Lambda1, while the u ¯d
pair from the weak process hadronizes into a meson–meson
pair together with aq ¯q pair with the quantum numbers of the
vacuum. The following ﬁnal-state interactions of the meson–
meson and baryon–baryon pairs are described in the chiral
unitary model that dynamically generates the a
0(980) and
/Lambda1(1670) states. From the line shapes of the invariant mass
distributions, the a0(980) and /Lambda1(1670) states are clearly
seen.
On the experimental side, the decay mode /Lambda1+
c → π+η/Lambda1
has been observed [22,39] and the branching ratio Br(/Lambda1+
c →
π+η/Lambda1)is determined to be (2.4 ± 0.5)%, which is one
of the dominant decay modes of the /Lambda1+
c state. For the
decay of /Lambda1+
c → π+η/Lambda1, the ﬁnal π+η and η/Lambda1states
are in pure isospin I = 1 and I = 0 combinations,
respectively. Hence, the /Lambda1+
c → π+η/Lambda1decay might be
an ideal process to study the a0(980) and /Lambda1(1670) reso-
nances. Future experimental measurements of the invari-
ant mass distributions studied in the present work will
be very helpful in illuminating the nature of the a0(980)
and /Lambda1(1670) resonances. For example, a corresponding
experimental measurement could in principle be done at
BESIII [40].
