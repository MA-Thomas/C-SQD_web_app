Evidence of the Anomalous Charge State 57Fe4+ in the Nuclear Decay of 57C03+
J. Fontcuberta and A. Fernindez
Facultat F't'sica, Uniuersitat Barcelona, Barcelona 08028, Catalunya, Spain

(Received 12 March 1986)
The first observation of the elusive Fe4+ charge state coming from the nuclear decay of "Co +
has been found in the Mossbauer emission spectra of 5 Co:La2Li05Co0504. A Ti-doped sample was
prepared in order to show that the Fe4+ fraction can be conveniently monitored. Both results were
predicted on the basis of the electronic energy-band scheme of these oxides.

Capture of an inner-shell electron by a Co nucleus
transforms the 57Co species into 57Fe~+ having an
inner-shell hole in its electronic structure. Such an
event is followed by an electron-vacancy cascade,
which generates in turn an Auger-electron cascade that
leaves the atom in an ionized state. '
The 14.4-keV y ray emitted in the final step of the
nuclear decay of the s~Co probe can be analyzed by
Mossbauer spectroscopy; the Mossbauer spectrum re-
veals the valence state and local environment of the
s7Co-atom probe about 10 ~ s after its decay. If the
Auger electrons ejected from the atom probe by the
intra-atomic cascade are not recaptured in a time
~ ~ 10 7 s, the probe atom exhibits the Mossbauer
spectrum of a more positive ionic state. Such a situa-
tion may occur in an insulator, and both 57Fe3+ and
s7Fe2+ daughter ions of 5~Co2+-ion probes have been
observed in Mossbauer emission studies of p.type
57Co0.2 The appearance of the higher charge state has
been related to the semiconducting properties of the
oxide.
In some recent papers, 4 we have reported the
MOssbauer emission spectra from 57Co probe atoms
substituting for nickel in p.type Co:La2Ni04. In this
case, the cobalt atoms capture the mobile holes of a
narrow Ni3+/2+ band, thereby becoming s Co3+-ion
probes. Nevertheless, no higher-valent s7Fe~+ -ion
daughter was observed. We pointed out that this find-
ing was consistent with a predicted placement of the
Fe4+/3+ couple below the Fermi energy in this com-
pound.
Several other studies of Mossbauer emission spectra
from s~Cos+-ion probes have similarly failed to ob-
serve any evidence of a s7Fe4+-ion daughter, s but no
physical reason was advanced as to why this should be
so. Therefore we used our analysis of 57Co:La2Ni04 to
predict that the Mossbauer emission spectra of p-type
Co:La2Lio sCoo s04 should exhibit a Fe + -ion
daughter. In this paper we present experimental data
that establish the correctness of this prediction, and we
show how Mossbauer emission spectroscopy can be
used to study the semiconductor properties of some
materials.
Following the procedure of Demazeau et al. ,6 we
prepared La2Li05Coos04 by dissolving stoichiometric
proportions of the corresponding nitrates in diluted
HN03. After decomposition at low temperature, the
sample was annealed in air for 10 h at 800'C. To
compensate for the sublimation of Li20 that takes
place at about 800'C, a 30% excess of LiN03 was add-
ed to the solution before calcining.
The radioactive probe atoms were introduced by
adding a drop of diluted s7CoC12 on a pellet of the
presynthesized oxide. After water evaporation under
an infrared lamp, the pellet was again annealed at
800'C for 6 h. Nonradiative samples were similarly
prepared for x-ray analysis.
The x-ray pattern, obtained with Cu K~ radiation
and a two-circle diffractometer, could be completely
indexed in the tetragonal cell (14/mmm) of the
K2NiF4 structure. The cell parameters a = 3.780(2) A
and c = 12.604(2) A are in close agreement with those
previously reported6 for this compound. No additional
reflections indicative of Li+, Co3+ ordering in the
perovskite intergrowth layers were detected; by using a
Guinier camera, Demazeau et a!P were able to observe
very weak superstructure lines.
In order to investigate the influence of donor impur-
ities on the Mossbauer emission spectrum, 1% titani-
um substitutions for cobalt were prepared; x-ray anal-
ysis did not show any structural modification due to
this substitution.
The Mossbauer spectra were recorded at room tem-
perature with a constant-acceleration drive system, a
single-line (SS) absorber, and a 200-channel analyzer.
