Abstract We study the decay /Lambda1+
c → /Lambda1π+π+π− , focus-
ing on the production of the /Sigma1∗(1430) resonance observed
by the Belle Collaboration. Interpreted as a dynamically gen-
erated state from meson-baryon interactions in the chiral uni-
tary approach, the /Sigma1∗(1430) signal is shown to be enhanced
by a triangle singularity involving intermediate K∗−, p, and
¯K 0 states. This mechanism leads to a sharp peak near 1434
MeV in the π+/Lambda1invariant mass distribution, in agreement
with the experimental observations, and predicts a secondary
peak around 1875 MeV in the π− /Sigma1∗(1430) spectrum tied
to the triangle singularity. We also estimate the branching
ratio of/Lambda1
+
c → π+π− /Sigma1∗(1430) to be about 3.5×10−4.T h e
results for the branching ratio and theπ− /Sigma1∗(1430) mass dis-
tributions are predictions of the theoretical approach, which
could be tested with reanalysis of existing data.

c → /Lambda1π+π+π−
[1]. The /Sigma1∗(1430) is a low-lying excited state of the /Sigma1

Triangle singularities (TS) are a powerful mechanism to
explain anomalous peaks in hadronic invariant mass distri-
butions. They happen in reaction mechanisms proceeding
via a Feynman triangle diagram when all particles in the
loop diagram can be on-shell simultaneously, and they are
collinear, plus the mechanism can proceed at the classical
level (Coleman-Norton theorem) [12], leading to a kinematic
enhancement that can look like a real resonance [12–20].
The theoretical framework employed to get the/Sigma1
∗(1430)
state is based on the chiral unitary approach with coupled-
channels dynamics, where some resonances appear natu-
rally from the hadron interactions [6,21–26]. In this way, the
/Sigma1
∗(1430) has been studied as a dynamically generated state
from π/Lambda1, ¯KN , π/Sigma1, and η/Sigma1interactions in S-wave, provid-
ing a natural explanation for its narrow width and mass near
1430 MeV [2,4,5,27,28].
Several recent studies have shown that TS mechanisms can
signiﬁcantly change invariant mass spectra and even simu-
late resonance-like enhancements that can be misidentiﬁed
as new particles [14,18,29–32]. Similar TS effects have been
found and well explained in processes such asB → Dππ or
/Lambda1b → J/ψ pK − [33–35]. Interesting is the effect of a trian-
gle singularity to determine the γp → K+/Lambda1(1405) photo-
production cross section at forward angles and small momen-
tum transfers [36,37]. Other works proposing the use of tri-
angle mechanisms that produce some resonances to enhance
its production via the appearance of a triangle singularity can
be seen in Refs. [19,20,38–44].
From the theoretical point, there is only one work tying
to understand the Belle spectrum and the /Sigma1
∗(1430) produc-
tion [38]. In Ref. [38], the whole spectrum is analyzed theo-
retically by including the /Sigma1∗(1385)( 3/2+) observed in the
Belle experiment, the hypothetical/Sigma1∗(1380)( 1/2− ), and the
/Sigma1∗(1430) (/Sigma1∗(1435) in Ref. [ 38]), which is also observed
by a smaller peak at the ¯KN threshold. The work uses the
method of effective Lagrangians, and the whole spectrum is
reproduced by ﬁtting about 19 parameters, a similar number
as also used in the Belle analysis. Two methods are consid-
ered to accommodate the /Sigma1
∗(1430)( 1/2− ): one parameter-
izing it as a Breit–Wigner, and the other one assuming that
this state is dynamically generated from coupled channels as
found in Refs. [ 2,5]. The cusp structure of the π
+/Lambda1peak
is shown to be better reproduced if the /Sigma1∗(1430)( 1/2− ) is
dynamically generated.
Our aim is different. Our goal is not only to calculate the
enhancement in the/Lambda1π+ invariant mass spectrum but also to
compute the branching ratio for the/Lambda1+
c → π+π− /Sigma1∗(1430)
decay and compare it with experimental data to further test
the role of the triangle singularity mechanism in this process.
We do not aim at reproducing the Belle spectrum, but concen-
trate only on the /Sigma1
∗(1430)( 1/2− ) peak, which we produce
via a triangle mechanism that develops a triangle singularity,
enhancing the/Sigma1∗(1430)( 1/2− ) production despite the small
amplitudes for this resonance stemming from the chiral uni-
tary approach. The interesting aspect of our approach is that
we can determine, not only the shape of the production cross
section in this reaction, but also the branching ratio for the
/Lambda1
+
c → π+π− /Sigma1∗(1430) decay without any free parameters.
On the other hand, due to the structure of the triangle sin-
gularity, we predict a particular shape for the π
− /Sigma1∗(1430)
mass distribution, showing a peak around 1875 MeV , which
could be tested through reanalysis of the Belle data. Obser-
vation of this peak and the measurement of the branching
ratio for this decay mode would shed valuable light on the
mechanism for the reaction and the nature of the /Sigma1
∗(1430)
state.

3.4 Discussion on possible alternative production modes
In the PDG [ 47] there are two /Lambda1+
c decay modes that
potentially can lead to the production of the /Sigma1∗(1430)
in the /Lambda1+

/Lambda1+

order of magnitude as the one for /Lambda1+
c → π+ K∗− p (1.4%),
which we have used as the starting point to build up the tri-
angle diagrams of Fig. 3. It is then right to ask oneself about
the possible contribution of these mechanisms to the produc-
tion of the /Sigma1
∗(1430). In these cases, the /Sigma1∗(1430) will be
produced through rescattering of π+/Lambda1→ π+/Lambda1,a ss h o w n
in Fig. 9a, b.
The ﬁrst thing to consider is that at the peak of the
/Sigma1∗(1430), |tπ+/Lambda1,π+/Lambda1|2 ≃ 1
5|t ¯K 0 p,π+/Lambda1|2, as one can see in
Fig. 1 of [55]. This already reduces the width of Fig.9a, b by
a factor ﬁve with respect to the mechanism of Fig. 3, which
involves t ¯K 0 p,π+/Lambda1. However this is not all. The strength of
a triangle diagram is tied to how close it is to having the
intermediate particles on shell and produce a triangle singu-
larity. It is easy to see, applying Eq. (18) of Ref. [ 14], that
the mechanism of Fig.9a is very far away from satisfying the
conditions of a triangle singularity, which makes its strength
small compared to the one of Fig. 3a. This, together with
the reduction of a factor ﬁve discussed above, renders this
mechanism negligible compared to the one of Fig. 3a.
The case of the mechanism of Fig. 9b is different. Once
again, the application of Eq.(18) of Ref [14] shows that this
mechanism also develops a triangle singularity, concretely at
M
inv(π− π+/Lambda1)≃ 1886 MeV , not far away from the 1875
MeV where the mechanism of Fig. 3a develops the singu-
larity (see Fig. 8). However, something subtle occurs in this
case. Indeed, the diagram of Fig. 9b is affected by Schmid
theorem [59], which states that if the rescattering is from one
state to the same one, the triangle mechanism is reabsorbed
by the tree level (same diagram without π+/Lambda1rescattering),
simply multiplying the S-wave amplitude of the tree level by
the phase e2iδ, with δthe phase shift of the π+/Lambda1→ π+/Lambda1
scattering matrix. This phase has no repercussion in observ-
able quantities, like cross sections or differential widths, that
we are looking at. This theorem has been scrutinized in Ref.
[60], showing that the theorem holds exactly in the limit of
zero width of the intermediate particle (theρ
0 in Fig.9b), and
also in the absence of inelastic channels (here we have the
π/Sigma1channel, with /Sigma1
∗(1430) decaying to both π/Lambda1and π/Sigma1).
However, even in these cases, the process “keeps memory”
of Schmid theorem and the effects of the triangle diagram
are visible in the coherent sum of tree level plus triangle dia-
gram, but strongly reduced with respect to the contribution
of the tree level diagram alone. This situation, together with
the factor ﬁve reduction discussed above, renders again this
mechanism negligible with respect to the one of Fig. 3a.
4 Conclusion
In this work, we have studied the decay /Lambda1
+
c → π+π− /Sigma1∗
(1430)( /Sigma1∗(1430) → π+/Lambda1)with a focus on the produc-
tion mechanism and nature of the /Sigma1∗(1430) resonance. Our
approach is based on the chiral unitary model, where the
/Sigma1∗(1430) emerges as a dynamically generated state from
meson-baryon interactions in coupled channels. We demon-
strated that the /Sigma1∗(1430) production can be signiﬁcantly
enhanced through a triangle singularity mechanism involving
intermediate K
∗−, p, and ¯K 0 states. The distributions show a
sharp peak in theπ+/Lambda1invariant mass distribution near 1434
MeV , which matches well with the structure observed in the
Belle experiment.

In addition to identifying the/Sigma1∗(1430) resonance, we also
predicted a clear bump in the π− /Sigma1∗(1430) invariant mass
distribution around 1875 MeV , consistent with the kinematic
conditions for the triangle singularity. Furthermore, we pro-
vided an estimate of the branching ratio for this decay mode,
obtaining a value of approximately 3.5×10
−4, which would
be most welcome to be contrasted experimentally.
Our results are a consequence of our interpretation of
the /Sigma1∗(1430) as a non-conventional resonance generated
dynamically by ﬁnal-state interactions, rather than a typical
three-quark excitation. The triangle singularity mechanism
plays a key role in enhancing the signal of this otherwise elu-
sive state. Future experimental analyses, particularly those
that revisit the π
+/Lambda1and π+π− /Lambda1mass distributions with
improved resolution, could offer further evidence for the pre-
dictions made in this study and provide deeper insights into
the nature of the /Sigma1
