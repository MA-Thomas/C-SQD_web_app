Abstract
The mass of the τ lepton has been measured in the decay mode τ → 3πν τ using a pseudomass
technique. The result obtained from 414 fb − 1 of data collected with the Belle detector is Mτ =
(1776. 61± 0. 13(stat.) ± 0. 35(sys.)) MeV/c2. The upper limit on the relative mass diﬀerence between
positive and negative τ leptons is |Mτ + − Mτ − |/M τ < 2. 8 × 10− 4 at 90% conﬁdence level.

Masses of quarks and leptons are fundamental parameters of th e Standard Model (SM).
High precision measurements of the mass, lifetime and the leptonic br anching fractions of
the τ lepton can be used to test the lepton universality hypothesis embed ded in the SM. The
present PDG value of the τ mass [1] is dominated by the result of the BES Collaboration [2]
and has an accuracy of about 0.3 MeV/ c2. The same level of accuracy in τ mass measurement
was recently reported by the KEDR Collaboration [3]. The data collec ted by the Belle
experiment allow a measurement with similar accuracy to the BES and K EDR experiments
but with diﬀerent systematic uncertainties; the latter experiment s analyse the cross section
for τ pair production near threshold while Belle measures the four-momen ta of the visible
τ decay products at a center-of-mass (c.m.) energy of √ s = 10 . 58 GeV. Eventually, by
combining these high precision measurements, we will signiﬁcantly impr ove the accuracy of
the τ mass determination.
Separate measurements of the masses of the τ+ and τ− leptons in Belle, allow us to test
the CPT theorem, which demands their equality. A similar test was pre viously performed
by OPAL at LEP [4] with the result ( Mτ + − Mτ − )/M τ < 3. 0 × 10− 3 at 90% CL.
To measure the τ mass, we use a pseudomass technique that was ﬁrst employed by th e
ARGUS collaboration [5]. This technique relies on the reconstruction of the invariant mass
and energy of the hadronic system in hadronic τ decays. The analysed variable is

In the absence of initial and ﬁnal state radiation and assuming a per fect measurement of
the four-momentum of the hadronic system, the distribution of Mmin extends up to and

ﬁt to the experimental Mmin distribution as an estimator of the τ mass, since the background
processes in the selected τ+τ− sample have a featureless Mmin distribution near Mτ.
The analysis presented here is based on 414 fb − 1 of data taken at the Υ(4S) resonance
(√ s = 10. 58 GeV) with the Belle detector at the KEKB asymmetric-energy e+e− collider [6].
A detailed description of the Belle detector is given elsewhere [7]. We me ntion here only the
detector components essential for the present analysis.
Charged tracks are reconstructed from hit information in a centr al drift chamber (CDC)
located in a 1.5 T solenoidal magnetic ﬁeld. The z axis of the detector and the solenoid
are aligned antiparallel to the positron beam. Track trajectory co ordinates of the charged
particles near the collision point are provided by a silicon vertex detec tor (SVD). Photon
detection and energy measurement are performed with a CsI(Tl) e lectromagnetic calorimeter
(ECL). Identiﬁcation of charged particles is based on the informat ion from the time-of-ﬂight
counters (TOF) and silica aerogel Cherenkov counters (ACC). Th e ACC provides good
separation between kaons and pions or muons at momenta above 1.2 GeV. The TOF system
consists of a barrel of 128 plastic scintillation counters, and is eﬀec tive in K/π separation
mainly for tracks with momentum below 1.2 GeV. The lower energy trac ks are also identiﬁed
using speciﬁc ionization ( dE/dx ) measurements in the CDC. Electrons are identiﬁed by
combining information from the ECL, ACC, TOF and CDC [8]. The magnet return yoke
is instrumented to form the KL and muon detector (KLM), which detects muon tracks [9]
and provides trigger signals. The responses from these detector s determine the likelihood

The square root of the variance of the obtained τ masses, 0.18 MeV/ c2 is taken as systematic
uncertainty due to the choice of the edge parameterization. This v alue exceeds the error
of 0.14 MeV/ c2, which is assigned as a systematic uncertainty due to limited Monte Ca rlo

In this analysis, we use the beam energy calibrated using the beam-e nergy constrained
mass of fully reconstructed B decays on a run-by-run basis. We estimate the uncertainty of
the beam energy to be less than 1.5 MeV, which includes the uncertain ty of B mass, tracking
system calibration and the eﬀect of the Υ(4 S) width. Using the Monte Carlo samples, we
ﬁnd that this uncertainty propagates to a systematic uncertaint y on the τ mass of 0.26
MeV/c2 under the assumption that the value 1.5 MeV is fully due to uncertaint y of the
beam energy calibration.
As a cross-check of the result obtained from the fully reconstruc ted B decays we analyse
the distribution of the variable ∆ M E = (M(µ +µ − ) − 2Ebeam) for e+e− → µ +µ − data events.
If a systematic shift exists in the beam energy or tracking system c alibration, we would
expect some shift of the maximum of this distribution from zero. A sm all shift of the
maximum of the ∆ M E distribution from zero is due to ISR and FSR. We ﬁt the ∆ M E
distribution to a sum of two Gaussians with the same central value mu ltiplied by a cubic
polynomial to take into account the peak asymmetry due to ISR. To check the consistency
of this ﬁtting procedure, we apply it to Monte Carlo e+e− → µ +µ − events with ISR and
FSR [11] that pass through the full Belle simulation and reconstruct ion procedures. The
∆ M E distributions for data and Monte Carlo are shown in Fig. 2 together w ith results of
the ﬁt. The reduced goodness-of-ﬁt values χ 2/N d f are 0.9 and 1.06 for the data and Monte
Carlo, respectively, where N d f= 51 is the number of degrees of freedom.

the 3π system in the τ decay we vary the mass and width of the a1(1260) meson in the range

this variation to be negligible (less than 0.02 MeV/ c2).

The pseudomass method allows a separate measurement of the mas ses of the positively
and negatively charged τ leptons. A mass diﬀerence between positive and negative τ leptons
would result in a diﬀerence in the energy between the τ’s produced in the e+e− collision.
This in principle makes the assumption Eτ = Ebeam invalid. The Mmin distributions for
positive and negative τ’s decaying into 3 πν τ are shown in Fig. 3 together with the results
of the ﬁt.
Good agreement is found between the distributions for τ+ and τ− . The mass diﬀerence
obtained from independent ﬁts to these distributions is Mτ + − Mτ − = (0. 05 ± 0. 23) MeV/c2.
Most sources of systematic uncertainty on τ mass aﬀect positive and negative τ leptons
equally, so that their contributions to the mass diﬀerence (and its u ncertainty) cancel. One
exception is diﬀerent interactions of particles and antiparticles in th e detector material. For
example, the numbers of positive and negative triplets for the selec ted pions are not equal
to each other. However, the description of this diﬀerence by the M C is reasonably accurate.
In the data the ratio of the number of negative to number of positiv e triplets is 1.034 while
in MC this ratio is equal to 1.031. To estimate a systematic shift in the m ass diﬀerence
between τ+ and τ− we compare the peak positions of Λ c → pK − π + and Λ c → ¯pK +π − ,
D+ → φ(1020)π + and D− → φ(1020)π − , D+

average relative mass shift from the decay modes listed above is app roximately 0 . 8 × 10− 4.
This value is used as the systematic uncertainty in the relative mass d iﬀerence between τ+
and τ− and corresponds to a systematic uncertainty in the mass diﬀerenc e of 0.14 MeV/ c2.
Adding the statistical and systematic errors in quadrature, we ob tain Mτ + − Mτ − =
(0. 05 ± 0. 27) MeV/ c2. This result can be expressed as an upper limit on the relative mass
diﬀerence [12] |Mτ + − Mτ − |/M τ < 2. 8 × 10− 4 at 90% CL. Good agreement of the Mmin
distributions for positive and negative τ → 3πν τ decays shows that CPT invariance is
respected at the present level of experimental accuracy.
To summarize, we have measured the mass of the τ lepton from the pseudomass distri-
bution of τ decays into three charged pions and a neutrino. The result is
Mτ = (1776. 61 ± 0. 13(stat.) ± 0. 35(sys.)) MeV/ c2,
in good agreement with the current world average and of comparab le accuracy. Independent
measurements of the positive and negative τ mass are obtained to test CPT symmetry. The
measured values are consistent and an upper limit on the relative mas s diﬀerence is
|Mτ + − Mτ − |/M τ < 2. 8 × 10− 4 at 90% CL ,
